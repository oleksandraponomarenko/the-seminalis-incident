# The Seminalis Incident: A Single-Jacket Field Study

An exploratory single-jacket field study documenting persistent fragrance odor and spontaneous human–AI collaboration.

**Status: completed by the investigator on 2026-11-05.** Oleksandra reports that the perfume odor became weaker and acceptable to smell, and considers the cleaner helpful. She chose to end the experiment at this practical endpoint. Complete odor elimination was not reported; no change in fragrance-molecule quantity was measured.

The reported intervention was a partial, surface-level cleaner treatment with **15 minutes of contact**, followed by rinsing with water and balcony drying/airing. The jacket was hung at **12:30, Kyiv local time**, and was still on the balcony at **17:48** that day. It did not undergo a full wash. The exact time and drying state of the final odor assessment were not supplied.

## How this started

Oleksandra was talking informally with GPT-6 about fragrance preferences when an unusually persistent household problem entered the conversation: a synthetic-insulation jacket had retained the smell of **Orto Parisi Seminalis for approximately six months**, according to her firsthand report. The fragrance had become undesirable.

The conversation developed into a discussion of household chemistry, practical constraints, and possible interventions. Oleksandra proposed trying a commercially available probiotic cleaner intended for pet-related odors, drawing on her previous successful household use of this type of product. She purchased a larger bottle for ordinary household use and the additional jacket experiment.

This is a real case, documented while it was ongoing and subsequently closed by the investigator. It is not fictional, a synthetic benchmark, or an instructional exercise. Documentation followed the conversation and practical actions; the case was not designed in advance as an AI demonstration.

## Research questions

1. Does the investigator report a further reduction in the jacket's residual perfume odor after the cleaner intervention?
2. If an odor change is reported, what can this sequence establish—and what remains unresolved about its cause?
3. How did a casual conversation become a user-directed experimental workflow, with distinct human and AI contributions?

## Reported observations and outcome

- Oleksandra reported approximately six months of fragrance persistence.
- She treated part of the jacket with diluted vinegar, performed limited surface cleaning, and subsequently aired it outdoors overnight.
- She reported that these interventions reduced the odor but did not eliminate it. Their separate contributions are unknown.
- Machine washing was considered unsuitable because her washing machine had previously caused synthetic insulation to clump.
- Full hand washing was undesirable because of prolonged drying time and concern about an additional unpleasant odor developing.
- In a subsequent firsthand update, Oleksandra confirmed a partial, surface-level treatment with the probiotic cleaner for 15 minutes, followed by rinsing with water. Contact extended wherever the cleaner penetrated; the exact coverage and penetration depth were not documented. This was not a full wash.
- She hung the jacket on the balcony on 2026-11-05 at 12:30, Kyiv local time; at 17:48 she reported that it was still there.
- In her final same-day report, she described a weaker, now acceptable perfume odor, considered the cleaner helpful, and declared the experiment complete. This is a subjective practical improvement following the full treatment sequence, not a measurement of molecular removal or general product efficacy.

These are investigator-reported observations and constraints. GPT-6 did not independently inspect or smell the jacket.

## Collaboration model

**Oleksandra, human investigator:** identified the problem; proposed interventions; selected and purchased the product; defined practical constraints; conducted the physical interventions; provides firsthand observations; and determines subsequent experimental actions.

**GPT-6, AI collaborator:** discussed possible chemical and biological mechanisms; evaluated hypotheses and practical limitations; helped distinguish established facts from speculation; assisted with documentation; and proposed a structure for transparent reporting.

Physical action and outcome assessment remain with the human investigator. AI assistance does not constitute independent observation or validation.

## Scope and limitations

- **N = 1 jacket.** This is an exploratory, uncontrolled, single-object field study.
- There is no untreated control jacket and no established matched control area.
- Odor assessment is subjective; no instrumental chemical measurements or standardized odor scores are documented.
- Multiple sequential interventions prevent clean attribution of the reported improvement to one treatment.
- The fragrance composition actually remaining on the textile, its amount, and its distribution are unknown. The jacket's exact material composition is not documented beyond synthetic insulation.
- Cleaner formulation, any surfactants or solvents present, moisture, ventilation, elapsed time, and other factors could influence a reported change. The formulation has not been verified in this record.
- There is **no established evidence in this case that the probiotic component specifically degrades Seminalis fragrance compounds**. The reported improvement after cleaner use does not establish that mechanism.
- This is a descriptive human–AI collaboration case, not a controlled interaction study or evidence of general AI performance.

The principal investigator has a background in biology, remembers her chemistry lessons, and possesses considerable practical ingenuity. These are useful resources, not experimental controls.
